## What it is

An indicator is a number that stands in for something that cannot be seen directly, and it is only as good as the choices packed inside it: who was counted, out of whom, over which months, and by whom [397]. Two figures can be compared only when those choices match. In a regional report they often do not, because the figures come from a state agency, a provider, a hospital, a national project and the local press, each with its own habits.

Four checks cover most cases. The first is the unit: a person served, an admission, an evaluation and a court petition are different things. The second is the source: an organization's own round figure, an official table and a press report may each count differently. The third is scale: a rate answers how common something is, where a count answers how many, and a rate built on few events will swing from year to year [397]. The fourth is the period: Alabama's fiscal year runs from 1 October to 30 September, so FY2025 means October 2024 to September 2025, and other figures use calendar years [397].

The regional map adds a fifth matter, which is how an estimate for a small area was made. Its tract layers come from two federal sources: sample estimates from the Census Bureau, published with margins of error, and model-based estimates from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [406] [407].

## How it works in north Alabama

Start with the unit. One person admitted twice in a year is one person and two admissions. WellStone's plan says its crisis center has "over 150 admissions per month," and the Department's FY2025 table gives 1,879 for the adult center [1] [64]. The Department's report gives a count for each provider in its tables and crisis-center evaluations in its narrative, and the pediatric unit's 520 has been read both as people served and as evaluations [64]. Commitment figures for Madison County show the same problem: over 300 people committed in 2019 by the probate judge's account, 309 petitions in 2019 and 414 in 2021 in one press report, and a peak of 589 involuntary commitments in 2023 in another [79] [80] [51]. Those are people, petitions and commitments, and the report does not read them as one series.

Next, ask who produced the figure. For fiscal year 2025 there are four figures for the people WellStone served. The Department's annual report gives 11,659 in its table of community mental health centers, with a footnote saying adult mobile crisis services are included, and 13,700 in its table of Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics [64]. WellStone's own plan says "over 12,000" and a May 2026 press account says "more than 13,500" [1] [48]. The column headings of the Department's tables were not captured, so the unit of its two figures is unconfirmed [64]. None of these sources divides its count between the two counties [64] [1]. The pediatric unit has two figures of its own: 520 for fiscal year 2025 in the Department's report, and 400 individuals in its first year in WellStone's account as relayed by the press [64] [69]. Figures the centers report to the state are not settled either: the state's evaluators found in September 2025 that the crisis centers' monthly reports contained inconsistencies that suggested a lack of quality control [62].

Rates need a look at the count behind them. Cullman County's preventable teen death rate for 2023, 73.0 per 100,000, rests on four deaths, beside Madison's 70.9 [149] [148]. The Cullman coroner counted 17 suicides in 2022 and 22 in 2021, according to the local paper, a difference of five deaths in a county that had 94,009 residents in July 2025 [207] [6]. Appendix A leaves a cell empty where the publisher suppressed or did not publish a value, and notes that its rates are not comparable with single-year state rates.

Then check the period. Cullman's drug overdose death rate is 26.4 per 100,000 in its hospital's assessment, an average for 2018 to 2022, and 35.6 in County Health Rankings, for a different period [83] [52]. The provider ratios also come in pairs: 474 and 568 residents per provider in County Health Rankings' March 2026 figures, and 500 and 609 for 2024 in the Kids Count profiles, which print no source or definition [52] [148] [149]. The report's own figure of about $49 of WellStone revenue for each resident sets FY2021 revenue over a 2025 population, and the report says to read it as an order of magnitude [66].

The regional map shades 117 census tracts, 95 in Madison County and 22 in Cullman [406]. One set of layers comes from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024: eighteen estimates for each tract, taken with their published margins of error [406]. These are sample estimates for small areas, so many tract values have wide margins of error, and one cell is suppressed by the Bureau [406]. The other set comes from the 2025 release of CDC PLACES: ten measures for adults, among them depression, frequent mental distress and loneliness [407]. The dataset's own description says the estimates are model-based and draw on the 2023 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (2022 for five measures), 2020 census population counts and American Community Survey estimates [407]. They are modeled values, not counts or survey results for each tract [407]. Even at county level the PLACES estimates carry confidence limits: 26.5% of Cullman adults with depression in 2023, with limits of 22.5% and 30.7%, and 22.0% in Madison, with limits of 18.7% and 25.3% [211]. The two ranges overlap [211].

## How this report uses it

Domain 6 collects the pairs of figures that disagree, and its table of what is not published shows what was looked for and not found, with a partial figure where one exists. Where the report computes a number it says so: the share of the two counties' residents that WellStone reached in FY2025, between 2.2% and 2.6% depending on which count is used, is labeled as the report's own calculation [64].

Appendix C lists the tables that need a check by eye. On page 29 of the Department's FY2025 report each heading is printed beneath its table, and one reading attached 6,627, the 988 call center figure, to WellStone's clinic row [64]. The report prints 13,700 for the clinic and keeps the page on its list until someone has looked at it [64].

On the map, read a tract's shade as an estimate of one of the two kinds described above. Because many tract values have wide margins of error, a darker tract is a higher estimate and not necessarily a real difference from its neighbor [406]. Neither set of tract values is used in the report's text, so a tract figure on the map cannot be traced to a sentence in a section [406] [407].
