## What it is

Alabama Medicaid is a fee-for-service program, which means it pays by the item [397]. A payer of this kind publishes a price for each billable service and pays that price each time the service is delivered [397]. Work that has no billing code earns nothing, so outreach, coordination and follow-up tend to go unpaid [397].

The Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic, or CCBHC, is a federal clinic model that pairs a list of duties with a different way of being paid [397]. A certified clinic must provide nine kinds of service, among them round-the-clock crisis response, outpatient mental health and substance use treatment, primary-care screening, targeted case management, peer and family support and services for veterans [397]. It must serve anyone who seeks help "regardless of their place of residence, ability to pay, or age" [374]. It may deliver some services through partner organizations, but it must itself provide 51% or more of the service encounters [373].

The payment is the other half. A clinic's rate is "prospective", meaning fixed ahead of time from the clinic's expected costs and not billed item by item afterwards [397]. Each clinic's rate is built from a cost report of the total cost of running the clinic, so it can carry work that fee schedules do not pay for, such as care coordination [397]. Alabama uses the daily version: a clinic receives a single payment for each day on which at least one qualifying service is provided to a Medicaid enrollee [371]. The rate is rebuilt from actual costs and visits after the clinic's second demonstration year and every three years after that [372].

One limit is written into the state's own manual. The daily rate "will not be paid for services provided to populations outside of those enrolled in Medicaid" [57].

## The key idea in one picture

Inside it sits a smaller box labeled *people enrolled in Medicaid*, with the note *Madison County: 18% of residents eligible at some point in FY2024* [58].

The lower layer is headed *what is paid*. An arrow drops from the smaller box to a box that reads *a day with at least one qualifying service: one daily payment at the clinic's own rate* [371]. A second arrow drops from the rest of the wide box to a box that reads *no daily rate* [57], and from there to *other money, or uncompensated care: nearly $3.4 million at WellStone in FY2025, by its own account* [48]. To one side, a small loop shows *cost report, then daily rate, then rate rebuilt from actual costs after the second demonstration year* [372].

## How it works in north Alabama

WellStone began preparing in 2022 on a four-year federal implementation grant [118]. The award record shows $3,974,261 paid in four installments and a project period that ended on 29 September 2026 [39]. The grant's abstract set targets of 1,700 economically disadvantaged adults served in Madison County, 1,200 mobile crisis responses and 500 primary care screenings [39]. By June 2024 WellStone said it had added primary care and an adult mobile crisis team [119].

Certification followed. WellStone announced in June 2024 that it and a Mobile center were the first two Alabama clinics in the federal Medicaid demonstration [119], and the Department's board minutes record that both were certified by the state effective July 2024 [42]. The Department's directory lists two WellStone sites offering the full range of clinic services, one in Huntsville and one in Cullman [5].

The measured results so far are about access. The Department told legislators in January 2026 that WellStone's wait for an initial evaluation fell from 18 days in January 2025 to less than one day in December, and the wait for a first therapy session from 33 days to eight [50]. These are two points in time for one clinic, not divided by county or age [50]. The Department's annual report counts 13,700 people served by WellStone as a certified clinic in FY2025 [64]. The federal demonstration specifies 15 to 17 outcome measures, and no result for WellStone was found in any document read [42].

The uninsured are where the model's two halves pull apart. Alabama Medicaid reports that 18% of Madison County's population was eligible at some point in FY2024, one of the five lowest shares in the state [58]. Among people under 65, 8.3% in Madison County and 11.9% in Cullman have no health insurance [6]. WellStone states that no one is turned away for inability to pay and reports nearly $3.4 million in uncompensated care for FY2025 [48]. Its strategic plan names "funding for medications for our adult clients with no third-party reimbursement" as an unmet need [1]. How many of its clients fall outside Medicaid could not be established from the documents read for the report.

Other centers are moving to the model. Mountain Lakes, which serves Jackson and Marshall counties in the ring, received federal approval in October 2025 [46], and its two sites are the only other certified clinic sites the Department's directory lists in the fifteen counties [5]. The Department's board was told in November 2025 that five more centers would become certified clinics in 2026 [115]. A September 2026 press report listed six centers receiving grants to pursue certification; Northwest Alabama Mental Health Center is on that list, and the centers for Morgan, Lawrence and Limestone counties and for the Shoals are not [375]. The Department says the process of becoming a certified clinic can take from six to eighteen months [46].

## How this report uses it

<!-- framing -->
Section 12 explains the payment and states the tension between the duty to serve everyone and a rate paid for Medicaid enrollees. The dependency map in Part IV carries the same point as a link from payers to the service continuum. Domain 2 asks what follows the federal grant, and reports that no document read says whether the Medicaid clinic payment, local budgets or nothing at all will pick up the costs the grant carried.

Domain 6 looks ahead to what the model's rules require clinics to report. As a certified clinic WellStone must report five measures it collects itself, among them time to services and depression remission at six months [173]. The Department's quality manual says it "will also develop an annual CCBHC Quality Report" [173], and the report found none published by 4 October 2026.

A few cautions help with the numbers. WellStone's rate is not published; the re-base memorandum names no clinic and no rate [372]. The Department's 13,700 for WellStone as a clinic sits beside 11,659 for it as a community mental health center in the same annual report [64], and WellStone's own plan says "over 12,000" [1]. A grant to pursue certification is an award. It does not make a center a certified clinic.
