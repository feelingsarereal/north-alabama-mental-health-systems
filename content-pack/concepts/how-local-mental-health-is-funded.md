## What it is

No single budget pays for public mental health care in the two counties: WellStone's plan lists its sources as federal and state funds, local government appropriations, Medicaid, commercial insurance, private pay, and other grants and contracts, and gives no amounts [1].

Each kind of money behaves differently. An appropriation is a legislature's permission to spend a set amount in a given year, and because appropriations are made a year at a time, a program funded once has no claim on the next year's budget [397]. Medicaid is public health insurance for low-income people, paid for jointly by the federal government and the state, and it pays only for people who are enrolled [397]. A federal grant pays for a named project over a stated period, and the federal government publishes each award [96]. Opioid settlement funds come from legal settlements with companies that made and distributed opioid painkillers, and nothing they pay for recurs unless it is later written into a regular budget [397].

One more distinction runs through Domain 2: capital money builds a building once, and operating money pays staff every year. The state's evaluation office shows the shift for WellStone's crisis center: capital was 74.5% of its spending in FY2021, and by FY2024 operations and administration were 87.3% [62].

## How it works in north Alabama

**State money.** Act 310 provides for public corporations, of which WellStone is one, to contract with the Department of Mental Health in operating mental health programs [21] [1]. The crisis center also began with a state decision: on 28 October 2020 the Department named WellStone one of the first three crisis center awardees, funded from an $18 million appropriation in the FY2021 General Fund budget [32]. The evaluation office says the state funds each of its six centers at $7,000,000 a year [62]. The enacted FY2027 budget carries $36,000,000 for the "Existing Six Crisis Diversion Centers" and has no line naming WellStone or Huntsville [93]. That would be $6,000,000 apiece if divided evenly, and neither document reconciles the two figures [62].

**Buildings.** The permanent crisis center cost a reported $10 million: $3 million from the state, $2 million from the Madison County Commission and the cities of Huntsville and Madison together, and about $5 million left to a capital campaign called Be the Rock [87]. The campaign had raised close to $550,000 when the doors opened [88]. Its page publishes no goal, no total and no donor list [89]. The pediatric unit cost $9 million, which WellStone credits to the Legislature, the Department and campaign donors without giving shares [91].

**Medicaid.** Since July 2024 WellStone has been a Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic [42]. For each day on which a Medicaid enrollee receives a qualifying service, the clinic is paid a single daily rate [371]. The rate is not paid for people outside Medicaid, and WellStone's own rate is not published [57]. Statewide, Medicaid spent $703 million on mental health in FY2025, and no breakdown by county was found in the agency's documents as read [58].

**Federal grants.** The federal grants database shows three awards to WellStone between fiscal years 2021 and 2025, totaling $9,575,938 [96]. A two-year community mental health center grant awarded in 2021 at $4,288,700 was later reduced to $2,502,021 [97]. A four-year grant to implement the clinic model, with a project period ending 29 September 2026, brought $3,974,261 [39]. The third award, $3,099,656 over a project period ending 30 July 2026, paid for assisted outpatient treatment [37].

**Local governments.** Huntsville's FY2026 budget allotted WellStone $900,000, from which the council moved $36,000 to another agency [98]. The city's FY2025 and FY2027 releases name no amount for WellStone [99] [74]. The City of Madison had a $45,000 appropriation for FY2024 on a council agenda in October 2023 [68]. Madison County's pandemic recovery plan lists a $1,000,000 grant to WellStone [92]. In Cullman, the city's FY2027 budget authorizes a community service contract with WellStone and the amount was not reported [100]. The press account of the county's FY2027 budget mentions no appropriation to WellStone or for mental health [101].

**Settlement money.** Huntsville's first allocations, in 2023, gave $850,000 to the Huntsville Hospital Foundation, $810,000 to First Stop and $750,000 to WellStone [40]. Madison County is using settlement funds for a mental health nurse in its jail [75]. Cullman County gave a one-time $250,000 to the Good Samaritan Health Clinic and $150,000 over three years to the district attorney's drug court [105] [106]. From a $3,000,000 state settlement appropriation, the Department gave WellStone $660,000 to expand staffing and hours at the 988 call center [45]. WellStone was not among the grantees in the third round of state settlement grants [108].

**WellStone's own filings.** The most recent year with Form 990 data on the public record ended in September 2021, with revenue of $25,940,539 and expenses of $20,991,369 [66]. For fiscal years 2022 through 2025 the database lists single audits and no Form 990 data, and the audits could not be opened [66]. WellStone's FY2021 annual report is the only place found where it breaks revenue down by source: $9,882,565 from state government, $8,654,700 from patient fees, $2,639,254 from local government and $1,026,303 from the federal government [95]. It reports nearly $3.4 million in uncompensated care for fiscal year 2025 [48].

## How this report uses it

<!-- framing -->
Domain 2 does the accounting and Section 12 explains the clinic payment. The gaps at the end of Domain 2 follow from one observation: capital was raised in public, and operating support sits in budget lines that are hard to find.

Three cautions apply when reading the amounts. FY2021 included local capital contributions to the crisis center, so its local government figure may not describe a normal year [95]. The City of Madison's $45,000 comes from an agenda packet, and the vote itself was not read [68]. The report's own benchmark, about $49 of WellStone revenue for each resident of the two counties, sets a 2021 figure over a 2025 population and is an order of magnitude only [66].

The grants raise a question the report cannot answer. On their stated periods the awards for the clinic model and for outpatient commitment ended in September and July 2026 [39] [37]. The record for the outpatient award leaves open whether that program continues at the same scale [37].
