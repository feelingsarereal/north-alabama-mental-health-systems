## What it is

A service area, or catchment, is the set of counties an organization is assigned to serve [397]. It shows who is responsible for an area and says nothing about how far a resident has to travel [397]. This report looked for WellStone's service area in the public record and found three different answers.

The first answer is two counties. The Department of Mental Health's county-by-county emergency list assigns Madison and Cullman, and only those two, to WellStone as their community mental health center [2]. Centers of this kind are what the Department calls "the designated points of entry" for mental health and substance use services [21]. WellStone's own strategic plan says it "serves the residents of Madison and Cullman Counties" [1], and the Department's directory of Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics lists full-scope clinic sites at its Huntsville and Cullman campuses [5].

The second answer is fifteen. The Department's Crisis Centers page lists fifteen counties under "Serving" for WellStone Emergency Services and WellStone Pediatric Crisis Care, which share one address in Huntsville [3]. The list is Madison and Cullman plus Cherokee, DeKalb, Etowah, Fayette, Jackson, Lamar, Lawrence, Limestone, Marion, Marshall, Morgan, Walker and Winston [3]. Two other Department pages give the same fifteen, and one of them describes the thirteen as "surrounding counties" [7] [8]. For routine care those thirteen are assigned to four other community mental health centers [2].

The third answer is "Region 1", a label that sits on three different maps. The Department's mental illness services map draws a Region 1 of twenty counties and seven centers; its county membership is printed only in the map image, and this report relies on the statewide edition's reading of it [9]. The Department's Regional Offices page lists a Region I Community Services office in Decatur covering thirteen counties [10]. They include Madison and Cullman and leave Fayette, Lamar, Marion, Walker and Winston to a Region II office in Tuscaloosa [10]. The crisis intervention training program run by NAMI Alabama has a Region 1 of its own, with eighteen north Alabama counties [11].

## How it works in north Alabama

The two maps differ most in how many people they hold. Summed from the Census Bureau's July 2025 county estimates, the core had 527,525 residents and the ring 792,981; both totals are this report's arithmetic [6]. Three of every five residents of the crisis catchment therefore live outside the two counties whose local governments appoint WellStone's board [6] [1]. That board has eighteen members, named by the City of Huntsville, the Madison County Commission, the City of Cullman and the Cullman County Commission [1].

The ring's routine care is divided among four centers. The Department assigns Jackson and Marshall to Mountain Lakes Behavioral Healthcare; Lawrence, Limestone and Morgan to the Mental Health Center of North Central Alabama; Cherokee, DeKalb and Etowah to CED Mental Health Center; and Fayette, Lamar, Marion, Walker and Winston to Northwest Alabama Mental Health Center [2]. On its southern side the catchment meets counties assigned to the Birmingham and Tuscaloosa crisis centers [3].

Other regional schemes cut the same fifteen counties along other lines. They fall into three public health districts and six area agencies on aging [12] [395]. Huntsville's 2-1-1 center takes calls from Madison and five ring counties, while Cullman's calls are answered from Birmingham [13]. Even the two core counties part company. Madison shares a homelessness continuum with Limestone and Morgan [311], Cullman belongs to a Balance of State continuum of 37 counties [312], and the two sit in different councils of governments [395]. Medicaid's care coordination map does keep them together, in a Northeast region with Jackson, Limestone, Marshall and Morgan [368].

One map could not be found at all. One of Alabama's four in-state 988 call centers is at WellStone, and the Department's 988 page gives no county coverage for any of them [4]. No press report read for this edition lists the counties the Huntsville center answers [14]. WellStone has said that a Cullman caller to 988 is connected to the Huntsville center [41].

## How this report uses it

<!-- framing -->
The report settles its vocabulary early. "The core" means Madison and Cullman, and "the ring" means the other thirteen counties. Region 1 is used only as a benchmark, and always with its size stated. Domains 1 to 8 and Sections 9 to 12 describe the core. Section 13 sketches the ring in outline and does not examine its courts, schools, hospitals or housing.

Before comparing any two regional figures, a reader can ask four things. The first is which map the figure sits on. The Department counts 1,879 people served at the adult crisis center in FY2025, a figure that covers all fifteen counties, and 11,659 served by WellStone as a community mental health center, the role it holds for two [64] [2]. The second is whether the figure is split by county, and the Department's FY2025 rows for WellStone are not [64]. The third is how large the Region 1 behind a benchmark is, since it may hold twenty, thirteen or eighteen counties [9] [10] [11]. The fourth is whether a figure with a familiar name describes the same counties. The 718 homeless people counted in January 2025, for instance, were counted across Madison, Limestone and Morgan together [60].

A catchment records an assignment, and use is a separate matter. The Department's FY2025 figures for the adult and pediatric units come with no county of residence [64], and the state's evaluators reported in 2025 that crisis centers may serve fewer people from communities outside the metropolitan areas where they sit [62]. Section 13 reads this as a sign that the ring may depend on Huntsville less than the map implies, and it adds that no number in the sources read can confirm or refute that reading.
