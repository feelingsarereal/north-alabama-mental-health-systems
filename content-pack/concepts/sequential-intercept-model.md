## What it is

The Sequential Intercept Model is a map of the points at which a person with mental illness can be turned from the criminal justice system toward treatment [397]. It lists six of them, numbered 0 to 5: community services, law enforcement, initial detention and first court hearings, jails and courts, reentry, and community corrections [397]. Communities use it to find gaps and opportunities at each point [397]. The earlier the intercept, the less time a person spends in the justice system [397].

The purpose at every point is diversion, which means steering a person away from a jail or an emergency room and into care [397]. Diversion is hard to measure, because counting one requires a judgment about what would have happened otherwise [397].

A few terms recur. A Crisis Intervention Team is a partnership of law enforcement, mental health providers and families, built around a 40-hour training course for officers [397]. A co-responder program pairs officers with clinicians [397]. A specialty court is a docket within an ordinary court in which a judge supervises a defendant through treatment and regular hearings [397]. Stepping Up is a national initiative to reduce the number of people with mental illness in county jails, and counties that join commit to steps such as screening everyone booked [397].

## How it works in north Alabama

**Intercept 0.** The Department of Mental Health lists the Huntsville crisis center as a place to which law enforcement and first responders in fifteen counties can bring a person in crisis at any hour [3]. WellStone's chief executive described its beds in 2022 as a way to divert people from jails and emergency departments [88]. As WellStone described it in 2024, a Cullman caller to 988 reaches the Huntsville center, which connects the caller to a local crisis team [41]. The state's 2025 evaluation publishes no count of jail or emergency room avoidance for any single center and found the centers' monthly reports inconsistent [62].

**Intercept 1.** In May 2021 Huntsville Police had 75 officers certified through the 40-hour course, and the city council voted to pair two WellStone clinicians with crisis-trained officers [34]. Two clinicians were riding by February 2022 [63]. No count of calls attended, arrests avoided or transports has been published by the city on the pages read [63]. Nothing on the Cullman County Sheriff's website shows crisis-trained officers or a co-responder [286].

**Intercept 2.** The Madison County jail is the booking point for Huntsville Police [287]. A county office runs a Mental Health Pre-Trial Release Program there for detainees who show or have a diagnosis of mental illness, and it publishes no numbers [288]. Huntsville's Municipal Court runs its own mental health court [289]. On the civil side, the federal grant for the probate court's outpatient commitment program set a target of 275 people enrolled, and no results were found [37]. In Cullman, a 2019 arrangement let a judge order a mental health evaluation, adopted after voluntary screening in the jail failed [291]. Of 98 inmates the jail referred in the first year of Stepping Up, four agreed to be screened [28].

**Intercept 3.** The Madison County Sheriff's pages publish no capacity, population or mental health caseload for the jail [65]. Its health care contract, renewed in September 2026, covers up to 1,235 inmates and adds a mental health nurse paid from opioid settlement funds [75]. Cullman's detention center has 553 beds, and the page describing its staff names no mental health staff [293].

Three deaths in the Madison County jail in 2013 led to federal lawsuits [295]. The complaints alleged that officers and medical staff failed to act as each person died of a treatable condition, and the defendants denied breaching the standard of care [295]. The cases, numbered 5:14-cv-01964, 5:14-cv-01959 and 5:14-cv-01309 in the Northern District of Alabama, were all reported as pending in September 2016 [296]. In the third, the court denied all motions to dismiss on 9 March 2015 [297]. That ruling let the case proceed and was not a finding that the allegations were proved [297]. How the three cases ended could not be established [296].

The circuit's site says a district judge has presided over Madison County's Adult Mental Health Court since 2015, and its capacity and completions are not published [299]. Cullman County announced a mental health court in May 2022, with WellStone providing services regardless of ability to pay [35]. Both counties were sites in the first year of Stepping Up, 2018–19 [28]. In 2020–21 the state's report completed no site summary for Madison because of staff turnover and inconsistent data [290]. Jail-based competency restoration runs in seven Alabama counties, and neither Madison nor Cullman is among them [64].

**Intercepts 4 and 5.** The Link of Cullman County won a $500,000 federal grant in 2020 for a reentry program, and no later report of whether it continued was found [304]. In Madison County, WellStone's jail diversion staff work to get people out of jail, into housing and to their follow-up appointments [112]. The state parole bureau runs one of its five day reporting centers in Huntsville [306]. Pretrial release, community corrections and misdemeanor probation in Madison County sit in one county office, and Cullman's community corrections program is a nonprofit [288] [307].

## How this report uses it

<!-- framing -->
Section 10 takes the intercepts in order and closes with a table that sets Madison beside Cullman at each one. The same services appear elsewhere under other headings: the crisis center is in Domain 3, and the probate court is in Domain 1.

The report's main point about this section is that programs are known by when they began. The co-responders have a funding vote in May 2021, Cullman's veterans court a first session in May 2019, its mental health court an announcement in May 2022, and assisted outpatient treatment a start date in February 2020 [63] [29] [35] [30]. None of those sources gives a count of people served since [63] [35]. An announcement is not an outcome, and several cells in the table say that current status was not confirmed.

The numbers that do exist need care. The savings WellStone's chief executive cited for jail diversion in 2024, about $300,000 a year, came with no method, year or count [112]. The only published inputs date from 2018, when the program had moved 20 people out of the jail [27].
