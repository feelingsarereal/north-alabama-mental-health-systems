## What it is

Civil commitment is treatment ordered by a court for a person who has not agreed to it, outside the criminal process [397]. In Alabama the court is the probate court of the county where the person is, the same county court that handles wills and estates [397]. Any person may file the petition [397].

A judge may order inpatient treatment only on clear and convincing evidence, a standard of proof that sits between the ordinary civil standard and the criminal one [397]. The evidence must show that the person has a mental illness, poses a real and present threat of substantial harm to self or others, will keep deteriorating without treatment and cannot make a rational decision about it [397]. An initial order runs for up to 150 days [397].

The judge must also choose the least restrictive alternative that is necessary and available [397]. One alternative is outpatient commitment, an order to follow a treatment plan while living in the community, which a judge may make only where a designated facility has consented to provide the treatment [397]. Assisted outpatient treatment is the name of Madison County's version: a court-ordered program for people with severe mental illness who can live safely in the community with treatment and have had trouble staying in it voluntarily [30].

State law changed in 2022, when House Bill 70 added a ground on which an officer can take a person to a mental health facility to be seen by a physician, namely being unable to meet medical and physical needs [36]. It also allowed courts to mandate outpatient care after release, and it took effect that July [36]. The Madison County probate judge helped draft the bill [81].

## How it works in north Alabama

Madison County's court publishes its instructions: a petition is filed in the county where the patient is, the judge orders the sheriff to serve notice of the hearing, and a guardian ad litem is appointed to protect the patient's rights [196]. In 2020 the probate judge appointed sheriff's deputies with more than 80 hours of crisis training as community mental health officers [197]. As the judge described the process in May 2025, a person held on an intent to commit has a hearing at about the third or fourth day and by then is at Huntsville Hospital or WellStone [51]. Inpatient commitment lasts up to 150 days and can be renewed to 300, which the judge called very rare because of bed availability [51].

State law has the probate judge order "one or more persons or law enforcement officers" to take the person to the Department of Mental Health or a designated facility [194]. The region once had a state hospital of its own: North Alabama Regional Hospital in Decatur had 74 beds when the state announced its closure in January 2015 [24]. With that closure, all remaining state hospitals were in Tuscaloosa [123]. No document read says how often a committed person from Madison or Cullman County is taken there or what the trip costs [194]. In May 2025 the court's director of mental health programs put the wait for a group home at 100 days or more [51]. WellStone's current plan lists crisis stabilization for adults under civil commitment as a priority for expansion [1].

The outpatient program began as a local arrangement: the court announced it for 3 February 2020, in partnership with WellStone and paid for from the passport application fees the court collects [30]. A person enters only after a commitment petition, an assessment, a physician's confirmation that outpatient care is appropriate, a written treatment plan and a hearing [30]. In 2023 a press account said the $35 passport fee paid for two case managers, one for the patient and one for the family [80].

A four-year federal award to WellStone, with a project period from 31 July 2022 to 30 July 2026, brought $3,099,656 and named the probate court and Huntsville Hospital as partners [37]. Its targets were 275 people enrolled and a 75% decrease in participants needing inpatient care or incarceration [37]. No results against those targets were found [37]. Whether the program continues at the same scale now that the grant period is over is not established [37].

How much demand the court faces is known only from press reports [79]. The probate judge said in February 2020 that over 300 people had been committed the previous year [79]. A January 2023 report gave 309 petitions in 2019 and 414 in 2021 [80]. A May 2025 television report said involuntary commitments peaked at 589 in 2023 and named no source [51]. Petitions and commitments are different counts, so these figures are not one series [51]. One account said about 63 people had been through the program and about 10% had returned to the court, against nearly 50% of people committed in the traditional way [81]. Another gave repeat cases as 86 of 414 petitions in 2021 against 142 of 309 in 2019 [80]. No independent evaluation was found [81].

Cullman County's court puts far less on the public record: its page lists commitments among its case types and publishes no procedure, forms or counts [77]. In late 2023 the sheriff told the state's opioid settlement commission that the county no longer had facilities to take people who need mental evaluations [78].
