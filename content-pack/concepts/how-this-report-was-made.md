## What it is

<!-- framing -->
This report was researched and written with Claude, an AI model, on 4 October 2026, working only from public sources: agency pages and documents, budgets, court records, organizations' own published reports and data, and local press where no primary document could be opened. The method has three steps, and each leaves a record a reader can inspect.

<!-- framing -->
The first step is research. The scope file lists 199 web search queries, run before and during two sweeps of research, and the pages they led to were read through an automated page reader. Each statement of fact was recorded as a finding: a claim together with the passage from the source that supports it. The publisher's own document was used wherever it could be read, and the press only where no primary document was reachable. In all, 1,375 findings were recorded and 759 were used.

<!-- framing -->
The second step is writing. Prose was written only after the findings were recorded, and the bracketed numbers in the text lead to the sources behind them. Values are shown as published, and anything the report computed is labeled as its own. Figures recalled from memory or estimated were excluded. Sentences marked as analysis are the report's own reading of the evidence.

<!-- framing -->
The third step is checking. Every block of the report was read against the source passages attached to it by independent checkers that did not write it. There was a first check of all 283 blocks, a second check of the blocks changed after the first, a third check of the blocks changed after the second, and a final consistency pass against the list of open items.

The method follows the statewide companion, which was checked claim by claim against its sources on 3 October 2026 [397]. That report supplies the frame used here and the checked county data behind the maps and Appendix A [397].

## How it works in north Alabama

<!-- framing -->
The record of the checking is the claims ledger. It breaks the 283 blocks into 1,497 entries, and each entry is one of four kinds. A verified entry, of which there are 978, is a statement matched to a passage in a cited source. An analysis entry (234) is the report's own reading of the evidence, including its arithmetic. A not-found entry (180) says that something was looked for and not located in the sources read. The remaining 105 are statements about the report itself, such as what a section covers.

<!-- framing -->
The checks changed the text. The verification record counts 215 problems found and fixed in the first check, 41 in the second, 5 in the third, 11 in the consistency pass and 9 in a second consistency pass made after the glossary and primers were written.

<!-- framing -->
What could not be settled went to the open items, which Appendix C prints in short form. There are 129, in four groups. Twenty are sources that could not be read, such as WellStone's audits for fiscal years 2022 to 2025. Eleven need a human eye; tables the reader returned inconsistently are listed there. Seventeen could not be confirmed, among them whether Cullman County's mental health court still sits. The largest group, 79, is headed "Not published, as far as this review found." Each item says what is missing, why it matters, where the review looked and how the item could be closed.

<!-- framing -->
The wording of that last heading is deliberate. Domain 6 says of its own table that each row "records what could not be located as of 4 October 2026" and that "no row is a claim that the number does not exist." Pages the reader could not open were recorded and never worked around, and archive or mirror copies of blocked pages were not used. Across the two sweeps 390 page reads failed, most of them refused by a site's request limit or its robots rule.

<!-- framing -->
The limits follow from that. About fifty pages of WellStone's website refused the reader, so much of what the report says about WellStone's programs rests on the Department of Mental Health's documents and on press reports. WellStone's audits for fiscal years 2022 to 2025 could not be opened. The reader returns extracts, so quoted passages are its rendering of a document and a few tables need checking by eye.

County figures were not fetched afresh; they come from the checked county table assembled for the statewide report [52]. The tract values on the regional map were retrieved separately on 4 October 2026 from the Census Bureau and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and they are not used in the report's text [406] [407].

The report is a draft for review. It has not been approved by Little Orange Fish, whose review it awaits, and it has not been peer reviewed. It is independent of WellStone and of every other organization it describes: none of them supplied material, saw the text or endorsed it. It was prepared for Little Orange Fish, which its statewide report names as the backbone organization for the North Alabama Mental Health Coalition [397]. The coalition is described only from public web pages, and none of its meeting records were used as sources.

## How this report uses it

<!-- framing -->
Every bracketed number leads to an entry on the References page, which gives the publisher, the date, a summary of what the source establishes and a note on whether it was read in full or in part. Of the 405 references cited when the checks were run, 336 were read in full and 68 in part; 92 are press reports. Where an organization is the only source for a statement about itself, the reference says so.

<!-- framing -->
Three habits help when reading with the method in mind. A verified sentence matches the passage recorded for it, which is a narrower thing than the source being right. A sentence marked as analysis is the report's reading and can be argued with. And a statement that something was not found is dated 4 October 2026 and limited to what was read, so the Open Items page is the place to see what a browser, a phone call or the next review might still turn up.

<!-- framing -->
The report invites the organizations it describes to correct it, and its figures are dated so that a later edition can show what changed.
